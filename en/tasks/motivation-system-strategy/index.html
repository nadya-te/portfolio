From isolated mechanics to a gamification strategy

2025–2026

Task

When I joined the gamification effort, the product already had a large number of game mechanics. Each solved a local problem, but over time it became hard to answer strategic questions:

  • which user needs the system already covers;
  • where to direct further investment.

Role

Initiated a discovery process that helped the team move from developing individual mechanics to shaping a strategy for the motivation system:

  • shaped an approach to researching the existing system;
  • brought in outside expertise to build a shared language;
  • organized cross-functional workshops;
  • synthesized the findings into product directions;
  • organized prioritization of initiatives together with the product team.

Growth areas identified

Strengthen competitive motivation

Problem: essentially, there was only one competition — the marathon.

Opportunities:

  • ratings;
  • competitions;
  • challenges;
  • in-game events.

In-game event "Zoomania"

Collectible cards for the event

Develop the rewards and resource economy

Problem: marathon points came first, other resources had limited value.

Opportunities:

  • new currency use cases;
  • in-game shops;
  • rare items.

Diamond shop

Strengthen emotional connection with the product

Problem: narrative, collecting, and personalization were underused.

Opportunities:

  • own character;
  • pets;
  • collections;
  • story scenarios.

Pet

Push notification about the pet

Avatars for marathon placement

Result

  • map of the existing motivation system;
  • shared language for discussing gamification;
  • identified unmet user needs;
  • prioritized growth directions;
  • launch of new features with a positive business impact.

This project showed my role as Head of Product Design: not to create individual solutions, but to help teams find the right directions and make better product decisions — together with the product manager, not in his place.
